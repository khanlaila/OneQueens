How to Get Legal Help

Gather Your Documents

Bring any IDs, visas, letters, or court papers you have. Don't worry if you don't have everything.

Call or Walk In

Many organizations offer walk-in hours. Calling ahead can reduce wait times. Interpretation is usually available.

Meet with an Attorney

A lawyer will review your case and explain your options. All conversations are confidential.

Follow Your Plan

Your attorney will guide you through next steps. Keep all paperwork and attend all appointments.

Know Your Legal Rights

If ICE Comes to Your Door

You do not have to open the door. Ask them to slide a warrant under the door — only a warrant signed by a judge allows them to enter. You have the right to remain silent and to speak with a lawyer.

ACLUF Hotline: 212-549-2500

If Stopped by Police

Stay calm. You must provide your name if asked, but you have the right to remain silent about your immigration status. You can ask "Am I free to go?" Do not sign anything without a lawyer.

Legal Aid Society: 212-577-2100

If You Receive a Court Notice

Do not ignore it. Missing court can result in a deportation order. Contact a legal aid organization immediately. You have the right to a lawyer in immigration court (at your own expense) but free help is available.

Immigration Court Info: 800-898-7180

Common Questions

Do I need to be a US citizen to get free legal help? ▼
No. Most legal aid organizations in Queens serve everyone regardless of immigration status. You do not need a social security number or legal status to receive help. Your conversations are confidential.
What if I don't speak English? ▼
Most organizations provide free interpretation services. Many have staff who speak Spanish, Mandarin, Bengali, Korean, Urdu, and other languages. When you call, say your language and they will connect an interpreter.
What documents should I bring? ▼
Bring whatever you have: passport, visa, I-94, work permit, any letters from immigration (USCIS), court documents, and a form of ID. If you don't have documents, still go — they can still help you.
Can I be deported for seeking legal help? ▼
No. Seeking legal advice is your right and cannot be used against you. Legal aid organizations are not part of the government or immigration enforcement. Your information is protected by attorney-client privilege.
What's the difference between a notario and a licensed attorney? ▼
In many countries, a "notario" is a licensed attorney. In the US, a notary public is not a lawyer and cannot give legal advice. Only trust licensed immigration attorneys or DOJ-accredited representatives with your case.
